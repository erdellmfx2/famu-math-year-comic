# Setting Bible v2: McCall-Hart University

## Fictional-Setting Notice

McCall-Hart University, its people, departments, traditions, buildings, teams, schedules, and events are fictional. The university is a composite Deep South HBCU created for this story. It is not a substitute name for Florida A&M University or any other real institution.

Real calendars, syllabi, athletic schedules, and academic-program references saved under `research/` are used only to create plausible rhythms. The story must not present fictional events as facts about a real university.

## University Identity

- Full name: McCall-Hart University
- Short name: McCall-Hart
- Initials: MHU
- Location: Bellwether, Alabama, a fictional river city in the Deep South
- Founded: 1887 by educator Lillian Hart and minister-printer Josiah McCall
- Type: Private historically Black university
- Motto: Learn deeply. Serve boldly.
- Students: About 6,800 undergraduate and graduate students
- Athletics name: Herons
- Mascot: Great blue heron
- Conference: Southern Collegiate Heritage Conference, fictional
- School colors: Indigo, copper, and cream
- Neutral colors: Cypress gray, warm limestone, and dark walnut

## Visual Language

McCall-Hart must not look like the FAMU visual material already in the repository.

- Architecture: honey-colored brick, pale limestone trim, wrought-iron details, deep porches, and shaded arcades
- Landscape: live oaks, magnolias, cypress trees, red clay paths, and a slow river beyond campus
- Light: humid gold mornings, storm-blue afternoons, and copper sunset light
- Banners: indigo fields with copper heron marks
- Student style: expressive contemporary campus clothing with indigo, copper, cream, denim, and neutral palettes
- Avoid: orange-and-green combinations, Rattler imagery, FAMU logos, real FAMU building silhouettes, and identifiable FAMU signage

## Recurring Campus Places

### Eliza Moss Hall

Home of Mathematics, Statistics, Computer Science, and the Quantitative Futures Lab. The building is named for a fictional early Black mathematics professor. Its limestone stair, copper-framed windows, old chalkboards, and newer visualization lab make it the academic home base.

### Jubilee Library

The main library. A 1930s honey-brick building joined to a modern glass reading room. Quiet upper floors, an active first-floor cafe, archives, group rooms, and a shaded rear terrace give it several distinct scene types.

### North Star Learning Commons

A modern tutoring and peer-coaching center. It includes open whiteboards, small coaching rooms, loaner technology, and late-night study tables. It is communal and practical, not a generic silent library.

### Hart Student Union

The social center of campus. It contains Copper Cup Cafe, meeting rooms, the campus radio booth, organization offices, and an outdoor terrace facing Cypress Walk.

### Founders' Bell and Cypress Walk

The central symbolic landmark. The restored bell marks convocations and commencement. Cypress Walk links the union, library, and Eliza Moss Hall. Major private conversations happen near the bell, but the landmark should not be overused.

### Henry Boyd Stadium

The football stadium, known informally as The Marsh. Indigo end zones, copper accents, a low bowl, and live oaks beyond the north stands give it an original identity.

### Ruth Ellis Fieldhouse

Home to men's and women's basketball and volleyball. It also hosts major lectures, career fairs, and Battle of the Bands events.

### Meridian Diamond and Magnolia Field

Meridian Diamond hosts baseball. Magnolia Field hosts softball. The two venues share a spring athletics plaza, which allows overlapping sports and student-life scenes without reducing athletics to background score reports.

### Bellwether Media House

Home to journalism, design, campus radio, and documentary production. Julian Cross works here and performs with the marching band, making the building useful to both relationship and extracurricular plots.

### Porter Center for Enterprise

Business, entrepreneurship, actuarial networking, and internship events take place here. It gives Malik's career ambitions a physical setting outside the library.

## Traditions and Major Events

- Opening Convocation: Students cross Cypress Walk and hear the Founders' Bell.
- Indigo Night: Welcome-week arts, clubs, and music event.
- Founders' Cup: Early fall home football game and alumni weekend.
- Cypress Week: Homecoming, including service, step show, lectures, parade, game, and band showcase.
- River Classic: Late-fall rivalry game against fictional Bayland State.
- Heritage Tip-Off: Basketball doubleheader featuring the women's and men's teams.
- Founders' Research Forum: Campus undergraduate research event.
- Bellwether Juneteenth Festival: Community celebration with service and math outreach.
- Summer Scholars Showcase: Student research presentations at the end of the summer term.

- Undergraduate research matching

## Sports and Campus-Culture Rule

Athletic events must change a choice, relationship, schedule, or consequence. A game cannot appear only as a score update. Use athletes as complete supporting characters with academic lives. Feature football, women's and men's basketball, softball, baseball, track and field, and marching-band culture across the year.
