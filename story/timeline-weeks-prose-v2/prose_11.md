# Week Eleven: Cypress Week

Cypress Week arrived with a parade banner, three drumlines, seven group chats, and the collective belief that sleep was an event for less spirited universities.

By Friday morning, McCall-Hart had transformed. Indigo and copper ribbons wrapped the lampposts. Alumni posed beneath Founders' Bell. Food vendors lined the edge of Cypress Walk, and every open patch of grass seemed occupied by a service project, reunion tent, dance rehearsal, radio interview, or student selling a shirt that would become vintage as soon as the week ended.

Nia stood outside Hart Student Union studying the official schedule.

Homecoming service day offered five projects: packing pantry boxes, cleaning a river trail, tutoring middle-school students, assembling hygiene kits, and recording oral histories with alumni.

She wanted all five.

Keisha appeared beside her in track warmups. "Choose one."

"I have not said anything."

"Your face signed up for everything."

Nia pointed to the math outreach project. "One. I am choosing one."

"Proud of you."

"That sounded sarcastic."

"It was seventy percent sincere."

Nia wrote the service block in her planner and drew a neat boundary around it. She believed, with the confidence of someone learning the first layer of a lesson, that her overcommitment problem had been solved.

## Friday, October 10: One Project

The math outreach group met at North Star to build game kits for Bellwether middle-school programs.

Nia helped design a coordinate-grid treasure hunt. Students would plot points, solve clues, and uncover a final message. She tested the game with volunteers, caught two ambiguous directions, and resisted joining the neighboring hygiene-kit line when they fell behind.

"Look at me," she told Keisha. "Staying in my lane."

"It has been forty minutes."

"Growth deserves witnesses."

Julian stopped by with a camera crew to collect footage for the homecoming recap. He filmed Nia explaining the game and asked her to hold up the finished kit.

"Perfect," he said after the take.

"Do you have time to eat?"

"Later. Band rehearsal, alumni interview, media check-in, then step-show coverage."

"That is four things."

"Homecoming rules."

Nia started to warn him about overcommitment, then remembered the shape of her own planner.

"We still have Sunday evening?" she asked.

Julian checked his phone. "Absolutely. Seven o'clock. Just us."

He kissed her cheek and hurried toward the next event.

Nia returned to her one project feeling disciplined.

She did not notice that Julian's schedule had become the thing she was trying to manage next.

## Saturday, October 11: Maximum Revenue

Saturday's calculus workshop asked students to design the most profitable homecoming T-shirt order under production limits.

The selling price depended on demand. Printing had a fixed setup cost and a per-shirt cost. Storage space limited the maximum order. Students had to build a revenue function, subtract costs, find critical points, and test the feasible endpoints.

"Finally," Malik said, "a problem willing to identify its constraints."

Nia looked at him. "Was that a joke about our lives?"

"It was a statement about shirts."

"Sure."

With his analytics role reduced and his first make-up block complete, Malik had slept seven hours. The difference showed. He organized the variables, found the derivative, and paused before declaring the critical point optimal.

"We have to test the boundary," he said. "The unconstrained maximum may require more shirts than storage allows."

Amara checked the endpoint values. Nia sketched the profit curve. Their feasible maximum occurred at the storage limit, not at the theoretical peak.

Dr. Brooks asked Malik to explain.

"Optimization does not mean finding the biggest possible value in an imaginary world," he said. "It means finding the best defensible value inside the actual constraints."

The class was quiet for half a beat.

Nia raised one eyebrow. "Still only talking about shirts?"

Malik smiled. "Mostly."

The problem gave him something his real life had refused to offer: boundaries printed clearly enough to respect.

## Sunday, October 12: Homecoming Rules

Sunday evening belonged to Nia and Julian.

At 6:22, Julian texted that an alumni interview was running late. At 6:48, he said the band had added a staging rehearsal. At 7:09, campus media needed him to approve audio before a post went live.

**Can we make it eight?** he asked.

Nia agreed.

At 8:17, she sat alone at Copper Cup with two teas growing watery on the table.

Julian arrived at 8:31 wearing a media credential around his neck and carrying his saxophone case.

"I am sorry," he said, dropping into the chair. "This week is eating me alive."

"You said seven."

"I know."

His phone buzzed. He looked down automatically.

Nia moved the second tea toward him. "Are you here?"

"I am sitting in front of you."

"That was not my question."

Julian put the phone facedown. "Five minutes. I need to answer one production message after that."

Nia almost said no. Then she remembered arriving late to his showcase.

"Fine," she said.

They spent twelve minutes talking about classes and the service project. Then the phone buzzed again. Julian apologized, answered, and began discussing audio levels with someone named Marcus.

Nia watched the ice melt in his tea.

She had assumed Julian would understand her crowded schedule because he was busy too. Julian had assumed Nia would understand his for the same reason.

Understanding did not make either of them feel chosen.

## Monday, October 13: Brilliant and Unreachable

Battle of the Bands filled Ruth Ellis Fieldhouse with sound powerful enough to vibrate the seats.

The Marching Herons entered in indigo uniforms with copper plumes, their opening formation widening like wings across the floor. Julian's alto line cut through the brass. He moved with the same discipline Nia had first admired during summer rehearsal, every step sharp, every turn placed exactly.

Nia stood with Keisha and screamed until speaking became unnecessary.

Julian was brilliant.

After the performance, Nia fought through the concourse toward the band exit. She wanted five minutes: a hug, a private congratulations, proof that the person on the floor and the person in her messages still belonged to the same relationship.

Campus media intercepted Julian first. Then an alumnus asked for a photo. Band leaders called the altos into a debrief. Two first-year musicians pulled him into a group selfie.

Nia waited near the wall.

Julian saw her and mouthed, **One minute.**

Ten minutes later, a media coordinator waved him toward another interview.

He jogged over to Nia, breathless. "You saw it?"

"All of it. You were amazing."

His face lit up. A camera operator called his name.

"I have to--"

"Go," Nia said.

Julian kissed her quickly while people moved around them. A photographer captured the moment. Then he disappeared back into the crowd.

Nia remained against the wall, proud of him and lonely beside his success.

## Tuesday, October 14: Next Man

DJ began the homecoming game fourth on the receiver rotation.

By the second quarter, one player had aggravated a hamstring and another had taken a hard hit. The receivers coach pointed at DJ.

"Cole, X position. Know the adjustment?"

"Yes, sir."

DJ ran onto the field with seventy years of alumni noise rolling down from the stands.

His first route cleared space for another receiver. His second ended with the quarterback scrambling. On the third, McCall-Hart lined up near the twelve-yard line. The corner played inside leverage, expecting the slant.

DJ remembered the adjustment. He released outside, planted, and broke toward the back corner.

The ball arrived over his shoulder.

For a fraction of a second, the entire year narrowed to his hands.

Catch. One foot. Second foot. Boundary.

Touchdown.

Henry Boyd Stadium became sound.

DJ ran toward the student section, then stopped before earning a penalty and celebrated with his teammates instead. Malik jumped from his seat. Nia nearly knocked Keisha's drink over. Imani screamed with both hands around her mouth. Julian, in the band block, played through a grin he could not hide.

McCall-Hart won in the fourth quarter.

In the locker room, DJ received no speech about destiny. The receivers coach moved his magnet two places higher on the depth chart.

"You earned more reps," he said. "Now prove you can block when the ball is not coming."

DJ stared at the board.

Publicly, he had scored the homecoming touchdown. Privately, his reward was more work.

He loved both parts.

## Wednesday, October 15: Full Credit

The game's late fourth-down decision became the subject of campus discussion Wednesday.

With a narrow lead and the ball near midfield, McCall-Hart had attempted the conversion instead of punting. The offense succeeded and drained most of the remaining clock.

At a student analytics event, Dr. Bennett displayed the decision model. Malik's corrected module helped define the range in which the attempt had a favorable expected value.

When a student reporter asked Malik whether his model had won the game, he shook his head.

"No model wins a football game. Simone Hart led the uncertainty revision. Andre and Priya rebuilt missing tags and game-state splits. Coaches combined the analysis with personnel, field conditions, and what they saw during the game. The players executed the play."

The reporter tried again. "But your section supported the call?"

"Our section supported one option within a range. That is different from predicting the result."

Simone stood near the back, listening.

Afterward, she caught Malik by the door. "Good answer."

"It was accurate."

"It also gave people credit."

Malik looked toward DJ, who was retelling the touchdown to anyone who had somehow avoided seeing it online.

"The work got better when it stopped being only mine," he said.

Simone smiled. "Careful. You are becoming tolerable."

## Thursday, October 16: The Perfect Picture

Julian posted the homecoming recap Thursday afternoon.

The video was excellent. Service projects, alumni laughter, band rehearsals, step-show lights, DJ's touchdown, the fourth-down conversion, and Founders' Bell all moved together beneath Julian's narration.

The final image was the photograph from Battle of the Bands: Julian kissing Nia while copper light caught the edge of his uniform.

The caption read: **Homecoming is history, excellence, and the people who make every crowded moment worth it.**

Comments filled with heart symbols and declarations that they were "campus couple goals."

Nia stared at the image. It looked like a perfect week she had not experienced.

She found Julian on Cypress Walk.

"Why did you post that picture?"

His smile faded. "You do not like it?"

"It is beautiful. It is also the only private moment we almost had, and there was a camera in it."

"It was taken at a public event."

"That is exactly the problem."

Julian shifted his bag. "I thought it showed us supporting each other."

"I spent the week waiting for you. The picture makes it look like I got everything I wanted."

"You were late to my showcase."

"I know. I apologized."

"And I understood because you were helping people. Why can you not understand that homecoming is the busiest week of my year?"

"I do understand! I am saying understanding did not make me feel less alone."

Students moved around them, some recognizing the couple from the post as they argued beneath the real trees.

Julian lowered his voice. "Do you want me to delete it?"

Nia hesitated. Deleting the image would not give them the missing time.

"I want you to ask before our relationship becomes content," she said. "And I want us to stop using busy as if it explains everything."

Julian looked down the crowded walk. "I need to think."

"So do I."

They walked away in opposite directions while their perfect photograph continued collecting approval online.
