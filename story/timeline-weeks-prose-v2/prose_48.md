# Week Forty-Eight: The First Date, Again

Summer B began informally on Friday with a research transition exercise.

Dr. Okafor projected an irregular floodplain bounded by a river, two curved elevation contours, and a road embankment. Students needed to accumulate a risk density over the region.

The region was not a rectangle.

One set of integration bounds worked for part of it and failed for the rest. A polar description simplified the curved boundary but complicated the embankment. Rectangular coordinates did the opposite.

"The double integral is not the difficult part yet," Dr. Okafor said. "First describe where you are."

Official Summer B classes would begin Monday. The problem arrived early to establish the standard.

Before calculating what a region contained, they had to tell the truth about its shape.

## Friday, June 26: The Irregular Region

Nia sketched the floodplain three times.

Malik identified where risk density changed with elevation. Keisha overlaid the road network and found that one boundary in the simplified region cut through an actual drainage channel.

They could divide the area into vertical slices, horizontal slices, or polar sectors. Each representation described the same land while making different features easier to integrate.

"There is no single natural description," Nia said.

"There is a description appropriate to the calculation," Malik replied.

They selected a piecewise Cartesian setup for the embankment and a polar conversion for the curved flood zone. The final method looked less elegant than one integral and represented the region more accurately.

Sunday's date sat on both calendars.

Neither mentioned it during the research session.

## Saturday, June 27: Divide Without Losing the Whole

The team divided the irregular region into workable pieces.

One piece used bounds that changed with (x). Another was easier with reversed order. The final curved section became a polar integral with a Jacobian factor.

Keisha checked that shared boundaries were not counted twice. Nia verified that no gap remained. Malik compared the accumulated risk with a numerical estimate.

Different descriptions produced the same total within expected numerical error.

"That is deeply reassuring," Nia said.

"About integration?" Keisha asked.

"I am choosing not to answer."

They finished before evening. Nia resisted the urge to rehearse Sunday's conversation with Malik. Relationships were not oral exams, even if she had prepared several possible follow-up questions.

Keisha confiscated her draft list.

"You wrote date objectives."

"Only three."

"Go be a person."

## Sunday, June 28: No Laptops at the Bookstore

The bookstore occupied a narrow brick storefront in downtown Bellwether. New releases filled the front. Used books leaned at dangerous angles in the back. A cafe served tea, coffee, and pastries on plates that did not match.

Malik arrived first. Nia arrived two minutes later wearing copper earrings and the expression of someone pretending she had not considered twelve outfits.

"You look nice," he said.

"You also look intentionally assembled."

"Thank you, I think."

They placed their phones face down. No laptops. No project talk. No friends positioned nearby to provide reaction shots.

They browsed travel writing, music biographies, and a shelf labeled **BOOKS WE CANNOT EXPLAIN BUT LOVE**. Malik selected a strange collection of maps from fictional cities. Nia found essays about storytelling through food.

At the cafe, they talked about Gulfport summers, Huntsville family reunions, music they secretly disliked, and the jobs they had imagined before mathematics.

The date felt familiar because they knew each other.

It felt new because no crisis had assigned them to the same table.

When the bookstore closed, neither wanted to leave.

They left anyway, letting the evening end before exhaustion turned honesty into impulse.

## Monday, June 29: What We Admired and Misunderstood

Official Summer B classes began Monday.

After double integrals, Malik and Nia continued the date conversation during a walk, with no project work in hand.

Nia spoke about Julian. She had admired his public ease, creative discipline, and ability to make people feel celebrated. She had misunderstood public visibility as proof of private understanding.

Malik spoke about Simone. He admired her ambition, directness, and refusal to shrink a future to reassure him. He had misunderstood choosing a relationship as proof that unresolved feelings no longer required attention.

"I do not want us to turn them into people who prepared us for each other," Nia said.

"Agreed. They had their own lives. We mattered to them too."

Nia appreciated the sentence. It removed the easy story in which Julian and Simone existed only as lessons along an inevitable path.

They also named what they had misunderstood about each other. Malik had mistaken Nia's warmth for availability. Nia had mistaken Malik's patience for permanence.

The date had gone well.

The conversation did not allow that success to erase history.

## Tuesday, June 30: Not Proof of a Purpose

Nia stated her boundary directly.

"I will not become proof that your breakup with Simone had a purpose."

They sat beneath a shade tree near Eliza Moss Hall before research.

"You are not," Malik said.

"If we date, I do not want the story to become that spring ended so the right relationship could begin."

"Spring ended because Simone made a decision about the relationship she was in. London mattered. My divided attention mattered. None of that requires us to become a couple."

Nia nodded.

"And I do not want to be proof that ending with Julian was secretly about you," Malik added.

"It was not."

They agreed that any relationship between them had to stand on current choices, not retroactively justify pain experienced by four people.

The boundary was not romantic.

It made romance safer.

## Wednesday, July 1: The Bottleneck Beside the Fieldhouse

The campus emergency-tabletop exercise simulated a severe storm during a summer event at Ruth Ellis Fieldhouse.

The model routed vehicles toward two exits and recommended opening a secondary shelter when the main route approached capacity.

Then facilities staff introduced one ordinary detail the network lacked: delivery trucks occupied a service lane beside the fieldhouse during event breakdown.

The route narrowed. Pedestrians crossed the same area. Campus police temporarily reversed traffic flow.

The dashboard missed the bottleneck.

Expected travel increased sharply. One accessible shuttle could not complete its modeled turn. The secondary shelter recommendation came later than planners needed.

Keisha logged the failed scenario. Malik documented the risk consequences. Nia asked facilities staff how often the service lane was occupied and who controlled it.

They did not remove the trucks from the replay to make the demonstration cleaner.

The exercise turned a campus detail into a serious validation gap.

Their first date had gone well. Their model had not.

Neither result needed to become a prediction about the other.

## Thursday, July 2: Honesty Before Exclusivity

Nia and Malik met outside the bookstore for a shorter walk.

"I want a second date," Malik said.

"Good. I have already developed a favorable preliminary finding."

"That sounds dangerously close to project talk."

"I am adapting."

They planned dinner after the Independence Day weekend.

Neither asked for exclusivity yet. One date and years of history did not automatically create a relationship. They agreed on the immediate commitment: if either felt uncertainty, jealousy, pressure, or a desire to step back, they would say it before distance turned into punishment.

"Honesty first," Nia said.

"Exclusivity only if we choose it directly."

They stood close. This time, no deadline controlled the room and no project emergency waited inside the moment.

Malik kissed her.

The kiss was brief, mutual, and not a contract.

When they separated, Nia smiled. "Second date confirmed."

They walked back toward campus holding hands for part of the route, then released them before entering Eliza Moss Hall.

Inside, Keisha had a failed fieldhouse scenario waiting.

Ordinary work continued, which was exactly what they needed the beginning to survive.
