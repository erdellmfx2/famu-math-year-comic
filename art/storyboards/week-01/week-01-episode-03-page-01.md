## Panel Plan

### Panel 1: The Prompt

Wide setting introduction. In the Eliza Moss seminar room, a projected prompt shows abstract blocks and chart shapes only, no readable words. Students work in small groups. Malik opens a clean spreadsheet while Nia turns her notebook sideways and sketches families by a bus stop. Leave top-left caption space.

**Caption:** `A neighborhood food cooperative wanted to offer produce boxes at several prices. The prompt included a tiny data table and just enough ambiguity to start an argument.`

**Malik:** `We have forty minutes.`

**Nia:** `I know.`

### Panel 2: Meaning

Split visual disagreement within one panel. Malik points to variables on his tidy laptop model; Nia points to her quick people-and-bus-stop sketch. Their gestures mirror each other across the desk. Keep central balloon space open.

**Malik:** `The model needs variables.`

**Nia:** `And the variables need meaning.`

**Nia:** `If the pickup site is hard to reach, lowering the price might not increase subscribers the way we expect.`

### Panel 3: First Model

Quiet medium panel. For several stubborn minutes they work separately at the same table: Malik's table is neat and overly certain; Nia's drawing is thoughtful but incomplete. Then Malik rotates the laptop toward her. Use body language to show the concession.

**Malik:** `Suppose we label my formula a first model and list your factors as limitations.`

**Nia:** `Suppose we also explain what information we would collect next.`

**Malik:** `Fine.`

### Panel 4: Calculation Becomes Judgment

Wide presentation moment in the same seminar room. Their shared model is projected as large, simple graph shapes with a clear limitations side panel but no readable invented text. Dr. Brooks taps the limitations section. Nia gives Malik a small elbow nudge; he tries not to smile. Reserve top space for the professor's balloon.

**Dr. Brooks:** `This is where calculation becomes judgment.`

**Nia:** `I am celebrating us. Different thing.`
